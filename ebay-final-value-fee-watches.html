eBay final value fee for watches

eBay's final value fee is charged on the total the buyer pays, not on your item price, and the percentage depends on the category and on your seller level. Most sellers calculate it on the wrong number and think they made more than they did.

The three things the fee lands on

  1. The item price. The obvious one.
  2. Shipping and handling the buyer paid. This is the one people forget. If the buyer pays you 40 to ship, eBay's percentage applies to the 40 as well, even though that money only ever passed through you.
  3. A fixed per-order fee. A flat charge that applies to the order regardless of price.

Worked example

Every rate below is an example. Your category, your seller level and eBay's current published rates decide your real numbers, and they change. Do not use these figures as eBay's.

The mistake that costs the most

Applying the percentage to 6,200 instead of 6,245 gives 651.00 rather than 655.73. Small here. On a busier account with paid shipping on every order the error compounds, and it always runs in the direction of thinking you made more than you did.

The mistake that costs even more

Treating the fee as the only cost. The fee is one line in a stack: landed cost, the buyer's premium if you bought at auction, shipping and insurance, any service or repair, duty if it crossed a border, then eBay's cut. A margin calculated on the sale price minus the fee is not a margin.
